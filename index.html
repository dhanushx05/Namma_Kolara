Tourism & Heritage

Must-Visit Places

Ancient temples, quiet hills and living history — all within an easy drive of Kolar town.

Temple

Kolaramma Temple

A thousand-year-old shrine to the goddess who gives Kolar its name — built by the Gangas and later renovated by the Cholas.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA
Temple

Someshwara Temple

An ornate 14th-century Vijayanagara-era temple with a soaring gopuram, protected by the ASI as a monument of national importance.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA
Hills

Antaragange

Known as "Dakshina Kashi", these volcanic hills hold a spring-fed temple tank and caves popular with trekkers.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA
Heritage

Avani

A Ramayana-linked village with a 9th-century Ramalingeshwara temple group and hills favoured by rock climbers.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA
History

Kolar Gold Fields (KGF)

Once one of the world's deepest gold mines and India's first electrified town — now a heritage-rich mining district.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, public domain
Landmark

Historic Mausoleum

A striking Indo-Islamic era mausoleum in Kolar, a quiet reminder of the district's layered history.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, public domain

Did You Know?

Fun Facts About Kolar

1902

Kolar Gold Fields became one of the first towns in India to get electricity.

1000+

Years old — the Kolaramma Temple, after which Kolar is named.

Top

Kolar's APMC yard is among Asia's largest tomato markets.

2 km+

Deep — some Kolar Gold Fields mine shafts, among the world's deepest.

6

Taluks make up the Kolar district: Kolar, KGF, Bangarpet, Malur, Srinivaspur & Mulbagal.

Silk

Kolar is a long-standing mulberry sericulture hub feeding Karnataka's silk industry.
